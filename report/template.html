An empirical study · formalization projects · two measurement tiers

Macroscopic quality signatures of Lean formalization projects

As AI-generated Lean corpora proliferate, we need statistical measures of quality that don't require reading the code — and that measure the artifact, not its author. This study implements fourteen metric families (reuse structure, hygiene, documentation, elaboration cost, and an amortization exponent that measures reuse as compression), extracts exact declaration-level dependency graphs from elaborated environments, calibrates every metric against declared high/low quality anchors, and asks which ones actually discriminate.

01

Verdict up front

Reuse is the signature — once it is measured as compression, not citation counts. The amortization exponent (§8) asks how many orders of magnitude of re-proved work sharing eliminates: Mathlib's average declaration sits on a 107.5-node inlined dependency tree, unreviewed dumps barely clear 101, and unlike raw in-degree the exponent cannot be inflated by splitting proofs into chains. Around it, the discriminating family is organization (cross-directory interweaving), process discipline (docstrings, granular imports, sorry hygiene) and economy (duplication). Raw reuse counts stay a coin flip, and the heartbeats hypothesis inverts — elaboration cost tracks mathematical effort, not quality. The ranking these checks produce is not an authorship ranking: TauCeti and LeanPool land inside the human band and the dumps at the bottom. And the corpus's natural A/B experiment cuts the other way: Math Inc's Gauss PR against the community Sphere Packing project tops every mechanical check — sorry-free, documented, deduplicated, organized — while community review still rejected it on definition quality and API design. These metrics are a floor, not a certificate, and modern AI pipelines have crossed the floor (§11–12).

02

The corpus

Twenty-two corpora — formalization projects plus a declared slop calibration set. Compiler internals, teaching games and statements-only conjecture lists are excluded by design, and so are SciLean and Tao's Analysis I: their sorry_proof and exercises-for-the-reader conventions are deliberate choices that hygiene checks misread. The corpus spans Mathlib and its reviewed satellites (FLT, PFR, AddCombi, Carleson, PNT+, CSLib, Physlib, Equational Theories, the community Sphere Packing project), mixed-authorship pools (LeanPool — 65 human / 38 AI / 20 mixed projects), curated AI projects (TauCeti, Math Inc's StrongPNT), low-curation AI corpora (Meta ATLAS, Seed-Prover, DeepMind superhuman, OpenAI's Erdős-90, Clawristotle, and Math Inc's Gauss PR measured separately against the community Sphere Packing repo it targeted), and three community-flagged slop repos for calibration (Pedigree Polytopes' axiomatized P=NP, Rubik Cube Group, GBLean). Provenance is shown as color, but it is not the analysis axis: the question is what separates low-quality from high-quality corpora, whoever wrote them.

Where the repo could be built, metrics come from the exact tier: a Lean metaprogram walks the elaborated environment and reads getUsedConstants off every declaration's type and value — capturing instance uses, notation-mediated references, and dot-notation that textual analysis cannot see. Repos that ship no lakefile (the proof dumps) use the textual tier, an approximate name-resolution parser validated against the exact tier in §9.

Where the reuse lives

A reference in a statement (a type) or a definition body is load-bearing: it shapes the API others must build on. A reference inside a proof is erasable. The shape of the graph

Two structural measures. Depth: the longest internal chain of declarations each building on the previous — layered theory versus a bag of independent facts. Concentration: how much of all reuse is absorbed by the top 1% of declarations. Both discriminate, in opposite directions: real libraries are deep and spread their reuse; dumps are shallow, or deep only inside single-file towers.

08

Reuse as compression: the amortization exponent

If reuse is Mathlib's central design philosophy, the right measurement is not how often a declaration is cited but how much work sharing avoids. Define the fully-inlined cost of a declaration recursively — cost(v) = 1 + Σ cost(deps), with repetition — the size its dependency tree would have if every lemma were re-proved at every use site. In a flat corpus this is chain length; in a shared library it grows exponentially, so we report log₁₀ cost: the number of orders of magnitude of duplication that sharing eliminates.

Amortization exponent (log₁₀ of fully-inlined dependency-tree size)

Dot = mean over declarations; tick = p90. A value of 7 means the average declaration sits on a tree 10⁷ nodes large if nothing were shared.
09

Hygiene: duplication and sorry

Duplication is anti-reuse: the same normalized declaration body appearing more than once. And a sorry is an unproved claim — fine in a statements-only conjecture list, disqualifying in a corpus presented as proven mathematics.

Exact-duplicate declaration bodies

Share of whitespace-normalized bodies (≥60 chars) appearing more than once.

Sorried theorems

Share of theorems whose proof (transitively) uses sorryAx — exact tier — or contains a sorry token — textual tier.

Docstring coverage on definitions

Share of public defs/structures/classes carrying a /-- ... -/ docstring. Mathlib enforces this by linter — a process signal.

Elaboration cost per kLOC (heartbeats proxy)

10

Classical software metrics, adapted

Software engineering has measured code quality for fifty years; the honest summary of that literature is that volume-derived indices predict quality poorly — McCabe's cyclomatic complexity rests on weak theoretical footing, and the composite Maintainability Index is widely criticized for averaging away the power-law tails where risk lives — while process signals (churn, review activity) and threshold "risk profiles" (the SIG maintainability model) hold up empirically. The adaptations below follow that lesson: keep the simple, distribution-aware checks; skip the composite indices.

Which metrics discriminate quality?

Calibration by declared anchors: high = maintainer-reviewed, Mathlib-integration-bound projects (Mathlib, FLT, PFR, AddCombi, Carleson); low = unreviewed dumps or corpora shipping thousands of sorries (Seed-Prover, superhuman, ATLAS). For each metric: anchor medians, the median of all unanchored repos, and the AUC of separating high from low anchors by that metric alone (1.0 = perfect, 0.5 = coin flip). What expert review sees that these metrics don't

Ilin & Nugent's expert-review case study (arXiv:2606.13925) examined a semi-autonomous formalization of Grothendieck's vanishing theorem — a project that is in this corpus (half of Clawristotle) — and found that compiling, sorry-free code still failed expert review on four axes. That gives us a rare labeled instance to test against:

Forensics: what does measurably distinguish the Gauss PR?

Since the Gauss PR tops the headline checks, we hunted for residual quantitative tells against its own target repo and the human research projects. Two survive: Mathlib-vocabulary poverty per citation — it draws on the narrowest set of distinct Mathlib lemmas per 1,000 references in the corpus, the signature of hammering workhorse lemmas instead of finding the precise API — and a heavier proof-length tail than the community repo it targets. Statement duplication, branching, and chain-shape all look clean.

The scorecard is humbling and confirms the paper's thesis: Clawristotle lands mid-pack on our composite — structural metrics see its clean file-level organization and zero duplication, and are blind to the definition-quality and API-design deficiencies the reviewers found. Two of four issue classes are partially visible (organization; reusability via never-reused definitions), two are invisible (definition quality, theorem generality). Mechanically checkable metrics bound quality from below; they do not certify it. The GitClear findings on AI-assisted code — clone blocks up 4–8×, cross-file calls down 35% — are the software-engineering shadow of the same phenomenon, and match the two metrics this study found most discriminating.
