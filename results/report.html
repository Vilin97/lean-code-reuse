An empirical study · 20 repositories · two measurement tiers

Code reuse as a macroscopic signature of Lean library quality

As AI-generated Lean corpora proliferate, we need statistical measures of library quality that don't require reading the code. This study implements nine families of reuse metrics, extracts exact declaration-level dependency graphs from the elaborated environments of built libraries, and asks which metrics actually separate mature, human-curated libraries from AI-generated ones.

01

Verdict up front

The headline hypothesis survives in refined form: reuse separates libraries from AI-generated corpora — but only when you ask where the reuse lives, not how much of it there is. Raw reuse counts are a coin flip (a bespoke proof split into single-use lemmas chains as densely as a library), and statement-level shares actually invert. What no AI corpus in this study reproduces — curated or not — is Mathlib-style cross-directory interweaving; what the uncurated ones additionally fail is a simple hygiene profile (wholesale imports, sorries, duplicates, one-file silos). The curated AI libraries (TauCeti, LeanPool) otherwise land inside the human range, exactly matching their reputation as the high-quality end of AI formalization.

02

The corpus

Twenty repositories spanning a provenance gradient: flagship human libraries (Mathlib and the toolchain core), curated domain libraries (CSLib, Physlib, SciLean), research formalizations (FLT, PFR, Tao's Analysis I, Equational Theories), controls (Formal Conjectures — statements only by design; NNG4 — a teaching game), curated AI libraries (TauCeti, LeanPool — AI-implemented but reviewed, and LeanPool absorbs several human projects), and uncurated AI corpora (Meta ATLAS, ByteDance Seed-Prover, DeepMind superhuman, OpenAI's Erdős-90 counterexample formalization, Clawristotle). The interesting question is not just human-vs-AI but whether the metrics track the curation gradient inside the AI cohort.

Where the repo could be built, metrics come from the exact tier: a Lean metaprogram walks the elaborated environment and reads getUsedConstants off every declaration's type and value — capturing instance uses, notation-mediated references, and dot-notation that textual analysis cannot see. Repos that ship no lakefile (the proof dumps) use the textual tier, an approximate name-resolution parser validated against the exact tier in §9.

Reuse degree: necessary, not sufficient

The base metric: for each reusable declaration, how many distinct other declarations in the same repo reference it. The tail tells the real story — every corpus has hubs, but only libraries have a thick middle: thousands of declarations each reused a handful of times across many files.

05

Where the reuse lives

A reference in a statement (a type) or a definition body is load-bearing: it shapes the API others must build on. A reference inside a proof is erasable. Layered mathematics shows up as theorems cited by other statements and definitions built on definitions — proof-dump corpora cite lemmas only inside proofs.

Standing on Mathlib's shoulders

For repos downstream of Mathlib, leaning on it is a virtue: more upstream references per declaration and a broader vocabulary of distinct Mathlib lemmas mean less reinvention. The scatter shows Mathlib references per declaration against the share of declarations never reused internally — the lower-right corner (heavy leverage, everything reused) is where good libraries live.

07

The shape of the graph

Two structural measures. Depth: the longest internal chain of declarations each building on the previous — layered theory versus a bag of independent facts. Concentration: how much of all reuse is absorbed by the top 1% of declarations. Both discriminate, in opposite directions: real libraries are deep and spread their reuse; dumps are shallow, or deep only inside single-file towers.

08

Hygiene: duplication and sorry

Duplication is anti-reuse: the same normalized declaration body appearing more than once. And a sorry is an unproved claim — fine in a statements-only conjecture list, disqualifying in a corpus presented as proven mathematics.

Exact-duplicate declaration bodies

Share of whitespace-normalized bodies (≥60 chars) appearing more than once.

Sorried theorems

Share of theorems whose proof (transitively) uses sorryAx — exact tier — or contains a sorry token — textual tier.
09

Which metrics actually discriminate?

For each candidate metric: the median for established human libraries versus AI-generated corpora, and the AUC of separating the two groups by that metric alone (1.0 = perfect separation, 0.5 = coin flip). Small samples — read these as effect directions, not significance tests.
